# 06 — Actuators, MEMS/NEMS, and Ultrasonics

## Converse effect
Applying an electric field to a piezoelectric material produces strain. This is used in precision positioning, vibration control, microfluidics, acoustic transducers, ultrasonic imaging, atomization, and micro/nano-electromechanical systems.

For a simplified longitudinal mode, S3 ≈ d33 E3. Actual displacement depends on geometry, constraints, field distribution, and nonlinear behavior.

## Actuator design variables
- Stroke/displacement and blocking force.
- Resonant frequency and usable bandwidth.
- Hysteresis, creep, and temperature dependence.
- Drive voltage and electric-field limits.
- Mechanical preload, mounting, and fatigue.
- Dielectric/mechanical losses and thermal management.
- Closed-loop position sensing and control.

High precision may require calibration and feedback because ferroelectric actuators can exhibit hysteresis and creep.

## Ultrasonic transducers
Transducers convert electrical signals into acoustic waves and returning acoustic signals into electrical signals. Material selection depends on frequency, bandwidth, acoustic impedance, sensitivity, power handling, temperature, and manufacturing constraints. Matching layers, backing layers, electrode design, and packaging are as important as the active material.

## MEMS/NEMS integration
Thin-film piezoelectrics enable compact resonators, filters, sensors, and actuators. Performance depends on film texture, thickness uniformity, residual stress, electrode interfaces, substrate coupling, and process compatibility. Report the full stack and boundary conditions for reproducibility.

## Reliability and safety
Evaluate fatigue under realistic drive cycles, dielectric breakdown, heating, depolarization, moisture ingress, and mechanical fracture. For ultrasound or biomedical systems, report relevant acoustic output and safety measurements; electrical input power alone is not a substitute for acoustic characterization.
