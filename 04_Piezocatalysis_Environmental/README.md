# 04 — Piezocatalysis and Environmental Remediation

## Mechanistic concept
Mechanical deformation of a piezoelectric solid can generate polarization and an internal electric field. This may influence charge separation and interfacial redox chemistry. Piezocatalysis uses mechanical excitation to drive or assist reactions; piezo-photocatalysis combines mechanical and light-driven processes.

Mechanisms depend on material, solution chemistry, dissolved oxygen, pH, light spectrum, and mechanical field. Do not infer a mechanism from pollutant disappearance alone.

## Targets
Research includes degradation of organic pollutants, dye removal, transformation of selected emerging contaminants, antimicrobial effects, and water-treatment concepts. Translation requires realistic water matrices, catalyst recovery, by-product analysis, and energy accounting.

## Essential controls
- Pollutant + excitation, no catalyst.
- Catalyst + pollutant, no excitation.
- Catalyst + pollutant + excitation.
- Light-only and dark controls for photo-assisted tests.
- Adsorption equilibrium in the dark.
- Scavenger/EPR controls where relevant.
- Repeated cycles and leaching/stability tests.

Report pollutant concentration, solution volume, pH, catalyst mass, excitation frequency/power, light wavelength/intensity, temperature, sampling, and analytical calibration.

## Degradation is not mineralization
A decrease in absorbance or parent-compound concentration does not prove complete mineralization or detoxification. Where possible, supplement UV–Vis or parent-compound analysis with TOC/COD, LC–MS/GC–MS product analysis, and toxicity assessment.

## Reproducibility
- Fit kinetic models only after checking model assumptions.
- Avoid direct comparison of rate constants when reactor geometry and excitation differ.
- Quantify energy input and energy efficiency where possible.
- Assess catalyst stability, structural changes, leaching, and reuse.
- Distinguish piezoelectric activation from heating or direct ultrasound chemistry.

## LDH-related research opportunity
Layered double hydroxides (LDHs) and LDH-derived mixed oxides/composites may be explored in piezo-assisted catalysis, but piezoelectricity must be demonstrated for the actual composition and phase. Do not assume all LDHs are intrinsically strong piezoelectric materials. Compare pristine LDH, calcined derivative, a confirmed piezoelectric partner, and physical mixtures under matched conditions.
