# Piezoelectric Materials: Reviews, Fundamentals, and Applications (2020–2026)

**Purpose.** A curated, research-oriented knowledge base on piezoelectric materials, their structure–property relationships, device design, and applications. It is intended for literature mapping, graduate-level learning, and identifying research opportunities.

## Scope
- Fundamentals: direct/inverse piezoelectric effects, symmetry, constitutive relations, and performance metrics.
- Materials: lead-based and lead-free ceramics, polymers, single crystals, thin films, nanostructures, and composites.
- Applications: energy harvesting, sensors and wearable electronics, environmental piezocatalysis, biomedical technologies, actuators, and ultrasound.
- Literature window: 2020–2026. Publication year and article type should be confirmed from the publisher record before formal citation.

## Quick scientific orientation
Piezoelectricity is electromechanical coupling found in non-centrosymmetric crystals and in suitably processed materials. The **direct effect** converts applied mechanical stress into electrical displacement; the **converse effect** produces strain under an electric field. Device performance depends not only on a material coefficient, but also on frequency, mechanical boundary conditions, geometry, electrodes, dielectric loss, impedance matching, and power-management circuitry.

Common material families include PZT and other ferroelectric ceramics, lead-free BaTiO3- and KNN-based ceramics, PVDF and its copolymers, piezoelectric single crystals, ZnO and AlN thin films, and ceramic–polymer composites. No single family is best for every application: high coupling, flexibility, biocompatibility, temperature stability, low toxicity, cost, and manufacturability can conflict.

## How to use this repository
1. Start with the fundamentals file and identify application-specific metrics.
2. Read the review list, then screen primary papers cited by each review.
3. Record complete bibliographic details and DOI before citing a paper.
4. Compare studies only when test conditions and normalization methods are sufficiently similar.
5. Use the research-gap document to turn literature observations into testable hypotheses.

## Literature-quality policy
- Prefer publisher pages, Crossref records, and DOI resolver links.
- Never infer a DOI from a title.
- Distinguish reviews from original research.
- Record online-first and issue publication years when they differ.
- Do not compare voltage alone as a proxy for harvested power.
- For catalysis, distinguish parent-compound disappearance from mineralization and toxicity reduction.
